Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated strong performance on a wide range of vision-language tasks, largely attributed to their advanced reasoning capabilities. Among various prompting strategies, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has emerged as a powerful technique for eliciting step-by-step reasoning, enabling models to break down complex queries into intermediate inferences聽 [ 40 ] . As a result, CoT has become a default reasoning framework across vision-language tasks, including complex visual question answering, referring expression comprehension, and reasoning segmentation鈥攁 task requiring both spatial understanding and logical deduction 聽 [ 22 , 24 , 6 , 8 , 4 ] . Reasoning segmentation has recently emerged as a key benchmark for evaluating how well models align visual perception with high-level reasoning, and recent approaches have incorporated CoT prompting to achieve highly competitive results聽 [ 25 , 10 , 23 , 7 ] . However, in line with recent evidence that additional reasoning does not universally benefit language and multimodal models聽 [ 42 , 35 , 37 ] , our empirical findings challenge this prevailing trend in reasoning segmentation: we observe that indiscriminate use of CoT prompting can lead to degraded performance due to overthinking , where the model engages in unnecessary or misleading reasoning steps that distort the grounding process and lead to incorrect predictions. As shown in Figure聽 1 , the model correctly identifies the target region without CoT, but CoT prompting flips a correct localization into a misgrounded prediction by inducing unnecessary spatial reasoning that dilutes the model鈥檚 focus on the query鈥檚 intent. These findings reveal that, in reasoning segmentation, unnecessary CoT reasoning can compromise the alignment between linguistic intent and visual evidence, often leading to performance degradation. We provide a quantitative breakdown of these failure modes in the supplementary material (Sec.聽 D.2 ). Building upon these observations, we propose DA-CoT (Difficulty-Aware Chain-of-Thought), a training-free reasoning segmentation framework that adaptively triggers CoT reasoning based on the intrinsic difficulty of each sample, as estimated from the model鈥檚 attention maps. As illustrated in Figure聽 2 , CoT-beneficial and CoT-detrimental cases exhibit markedly different attention patterns. Interestingly, when CoT is unnecessary, the attention map from the < < SOS > > token to the visual tokens (hereafter referred to as the attention map ) shows low entropy, indicating that the model鈥檚 focus is already well localized to the relevant region. In contrast, CoT-beneficial cases exhibit high-entropy attention, reflecting dispersed focus and uncertainty in spatial grounding. These findings suggest that the model鈥檚 internal attention entropy serves as a reliable signal for determining whether CoT reasoning should be applied. Leveraging this signal, DA-CoT effectively distinguishes cases that do not require CoT, thus avoiding overthinking. Consequently, by adaptively bypassing unnecessary reasoning, it significantly improves inference efficiency. Furthermore, to enhance the robustness of the proposed adaptive reasoning path, we introduce a Bidirectional Rethinking Mechanism , which provides a fallback strategy when the initially selected reasoning path fails to produce a valid output. In such cases, the model automatically reverts to the alternative reasoning mode (with or without CoT), mimicking the human tendency to 鈥渞ethink鈥?a problem from the opposite perspective. This dual-path reasoning grants the model a second opportunity to recover from potential failures caused by either overthinking or underthinking. Extensive experiments on Reasoning Segmentation and Referring Segmentation benchmarks demonstrate that our approach consistently improves performance, yielding strong results overall and demonstrating superior performance compared to prior works on Reasoning Segmentation. Interestingly, DA-CoT outperforms existing methods that enhance their CoT capabilities through Reinforcement Learning聽 [ 22 , 10 , 23 ] , highlighting the strength of our training-free adaptive design. Moreover, when integrated into existing learning-based reasoning frameworks, our method consistently enhances their performance, confirming its compatibility and general applicability. Comprehensive ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of each proposed component. We make the following contributions: 鈥?/span> We challenge the prevailing view that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting should be applied by default, revealing that indiscriminate CoT usage can cause overthinking and degrade grounding accuracy in reasoning segmentation. 鈥?/span> We discover that the entropy of attention maps reflects task difficulty, providing a simple yet effective intrinsic signal for determining when CoT reasoning is beneficial or detrimental. 鈥?/span> We propose a training-free adaptive reasoning framework that integrates DA-CoT (Difficulty-Aware Chain-of-Thought) and a Bidirectional Rethinking Mechanism , where DA-CoT selectively applies CoT reasoning based on attention entropy to avoid overthinking, and the rethinking mechanism enhances robustness by enabling automatic fallback between reasoning modes for recovering from both overthinking- and underthinking-induced failures. 鈥?/span> Our approach outperforms prior training-based methods on ReasonSeg, while achieving competitive performance on Referring Segmentation. Furthermore, it demonstrates lower inference time and plug-and-play compatibility across diverse MLLM scales and architectures. 2 Related Work 2.1 Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) Building on the success of LLMs, early MLLMs such as LLaVA聽 [ 20 , 21 ] and BLIP-2聽 [ 17 ] aligned pre-trained vision encoders with LLMs through visual instruction tuning. Recent models, including the Qwen-VL series聽 [ 2 ] and GLM-4.1V-Thinking聽 [ 9 ] , further improve multimodal reasoning through larger scales and reinforcement learning. These advances enable complex tasks beyond VQA, including the reasoning segmentation studied in this work. 2.2 Reasoning Segmentation The task of Reasoning Segmentation was first proposed by LISA聽 [ 16 ] , requiring models to generate segmentation masks from complex and implicit text queries. LISA pioneered the 鈥渆mbedding-as-mask鈥?paradigm, adding a < < SEG > > token to the MLLM鈥檚 vocabulary to grant it pixel-level generation capabilities. Subsequent works refine this paradigm for efficiency, replacing the external segmentation model with a lightweight decoder聽 [ 31 ] or prompting SAM聽 [ 15 ] with MLLM-generated points聽 [ 5 ] . In a different direction, RSVP聽 [ 25 ] presented a training-free two-stage framework separating reasoning from segmentation, leveraging visual prompting to utilize the MLLM鈥檚 inherent localization abilities. Also training-free, ThinkFirst聽 [ 13 ] prompts GPT-4o to produce a chain-of-thought description of the image that conditions a downstream segmentation assistant, applying CoT unconditionally to every query. Advanced training strategies have also been a key research focus. Seg-Zero聽 [ 22 ] demonstrated that reasoning abilities can be elicited via cognitive reinforcement learning (RL) without explicit reasoning data, achieving strong zero-shot performance on the ReasonSeg benchmark. SAM-R1聽 [ 10 ] also utilized RL, uniquely integrating SAM 2聽 [ 29 ] as a reward provider to enhance alignment between reasoning and segmentation. Recently, SAM-Veteran聽 [ 7 ] extended this paradigm by training an MLLM agent to mimic interactive segmentation through iterative mask refinement. While these training-based methods have successfully advanced the field, they all apply a fixed reasoning strategy. Our work departs from this training-centric approach to propose a training-free framework that dynamically modulates the reasoning strategy. 2.3 Chain-of-Thought in Multimodal Contexts Chain-of-Thought was a prompting technique proposed for LLMs to solve complex reasoning problems by generating intermediate steps聽 [ 40 ] . This method has been adapted for MLLMs. Models like VisionReasoner聽 [ 23 ] have sought to improve performance by generating structured reasoning processes, and other studies have explored integrating visual information into the CoT聽 [ 48 , 34 ] . While such efforts to enhance CoT capabilities have been active, the question of when to activate CoT has remained unaddressed. CoT is a powerful tool, but its benefits are not universal. Recent studies in NLP have pointed out that applying CoT to simple problems that do not require multi-step reasoning can induce 鈥渙verthinking,鈥?leading to performance degradation聽 [ 42 , 35 ] . This risk is acute in reasoning segmentation, where unnecessary CoT can disrupt the precise visual grounding the task depends on. Tian et al.聽 [ 37 ] recently showed that in reasoning-trained MLLMs, prolonged reasoning progressively weakens perceptual grounding, and mitigated this effect by retraining the model with vision-anchored policy optimization. In contrast to existing research focused on enhancing CoT capabilities through training聽 [ 37 , 10 , 22 , 23 ] , our work explores the problem of when to activate CoT. We analyze the model鈥檚 internal attention mechanisms to propose an adaptive, training-free framework that selectively leverages the strengths of CoT while avoiding its potential pitfalls. 3 Preliminary Analysis We observe, through empirical findings such as Figure聽 2 , that it may be possible to determine whether CoT prompting should be applied to a given sample based on intrinsic signals 鈥攚ithout requiring any additional training. To further validate the design of BiReT, we analyze whether its performance gain stems merely from taking an additional inference attempt or from the explicit strategy flipping mechanism. To this end, we compare BiReT against a fixed-strategy retry baseline, termed Unidirectional Rethinking ( UniReT ), which re-generates outputs without strategy switching (i.e., CoT 鈫?/mo> \rightarrow CoT or Non-CoT 鈫?/mo> \rightarrow Non-CoT). Following common evaluation practice, all methods in our experiments use deterministic decoding unless otherwise noted, meaning that the same input under the same model and prompt yields a single fixed output. UniReT departs from this default only during the fallback phase by re-decoding the same reasoning mode under varying temperatures t 鈭?/mo> { 0.5 , 0.7 , 1.0 } t\in\{0.5,0.7,1.0\} . Changing the temperature alters the token sampling distribution, so repeated runs on the same input can produce different reasoning traces and final predictions; we therefore report results averaged over 3 runs. We evaluate the effectiveness using the Valid Recovery Rate , defined as the proportion of initially failed samples that successfully yield a valid response after the fallback phase, and the Correct Recovery Rate , the proportion of initially failed samples whose recovered prediction achieves IoU > > 0.5. As shown in Table聽 11 , UniReT peaks at 38.7% Valid Recovery Rate and at most a 2.8% Correct Recovery Rate, whereas BiReT achieves 97.2% and 22.2%, respectively, with the larger overall gain. These results indicate that the key advantage of BiReT does not come merely from an additional retry, but from explicitly flipping the reasoning strategy. By switching reasoning modes, BiReT forces the model to approach the query from an entirely fresh perspective, and it is this flip, rather than resampling, that produces the correct recoveries. As explained in the main paper (Sec.聽 4.3 ), BiReT is automatically activated when the initial reasoning path selected by DA-CoT fails to produce a valid output (e.g., missing coordinates or empty coordinate fields). Such failures indicate a mismatch between the estimated difficulty and the reasoning path actually required for the sample. BiReT then provides a second chance for the model to generate a valid output by issuing a flipping signal , which switches the reasoning strategy from Non-CoT to CoT, or vice versa. C.1 Rule-based Failure Detection To make this trigger precise, BiReT relies on a strict rule-based failure detection mechanism rather than vague criteria. The flipping signal refers to switching the reasoning strategy from Non-CoT to CoT, or vice versa. If the flipped second pass still fails to produce a valid output, we regard the sample as a final failure of the model. As illustrated in Figure聽 8 , BiReT is triggered when: (1) the structural components of the JSON output (marked in red ) are incomplete, such as missing closing brackets, or (2) the coordinate values (marked in blue) are empty, even when the JSON format itself is valid. During the design of BiReT, we empirically observed a strong correlation between instances of overthinking or underthinking and the breakdown of the output鈥檚 structural integrity. As illustrated in Figure聽 9 , failures to complete the JSON format or omissions of bounding box coordinates serve as strong indicators that the reasoning process has derailed. This empirical finding served as the primary motivation for adopting our lightweight, rule-based failure detection logic. BiReT is executed at most once per sample. C.3 Design Motivation and Efficiency Admittedly, one could consider more sophisticated verification methods as the trigger condition for BiReT. For instance, the model could be prompted to perform self-correction on its generated reasoning chain and predicted regions via an additional forward pass, or a more capable external model could be deployed as a 鈥淛udge鈥?to evaluate the correctness of the initial output. However, such approaches inevitably incur substantial computational costs and latency overheads. In contrast, our lightweight, rule-based BiReT demonstrates its efficiency, restoring a valid output for up to 97.2% of initially failed samples purely through strategy switching, without requiring any additional verification forward passes or heavy external models. Consequently, this simple rule-based mechanism strikes an optimal balance, simultaneously maximizing both the computational efficiency and output reliability of our framework. As quantitatively demonstrated in Table聽 19 , integrating BiReT incurs a minimal latency overhead of 0.3 seconds per image (from 4.4s to 4.7s), successfully preserving the substantial speedup achieved by DA-CoT. However, forcibly applying CoT induces an unnecessary and convoluted reasoning process (overthinking). This can dilute the model鈥檚 focus or lead it down an incorrect logical path, ultimately resulting in a misgrounded prediction. CoT-Beneficial Cases Conversely, Figure聽 13 presents additional CoT-beneficial cases where CoT plays a crucial role in improving reasoning performance. D.2 Quantitative Analysis of CoT-Induced Failures Beyond the qualitative case studies of CoT-detrimental examples, we provide a quantitative error analysis that characterizes how indiscriminate CoT prompting degrades grounding. We focus on CoT-detrimental cross-over cases : samples that the model grounds correctly without CoT ( IoU NonCoT > 0.5 \mathrm{IoU}_{\mathrm{NonCoT}}>0.5 ) but fails once CoT is applied ( IoU CoT 鈮?/mo> 0.5 \mathrm{IoU}_{\mathrm{CoT}}\leq 0.5 ). We randomly sample 165 such cross-over cases pooled from the ReasonSeg val and test splits, and assign each to its dominant failure cause: spatial confusion (the prediction drifts to a wrong instance during spatial reasoning), semantic drift (the prediction shifts to a semantically related but incorrect object), visual hallucination (the model grounds to an object that is not present), and format errors / others (malformed or incomplete output structure). As shown in Table聽 20 , spatial confusion is by far the dominant failure mode (50.3%), followed by semantic drift (18.8%), visual hallucination (16.4%), and format errors (14.5%). This indicates that the unnecessary reasoning steps introduced by CoT most frequently derail grounding through spatial-logic errors rather than perceptual mistakes. The result quantitatively substantiates our central claim that overthinking compromises visual grounding, and shows that this degradation manifests through structured, spatially-grounded failures specific to pixel-level grounding鈥攄irectly motivating our difficulty-aware, adaptive CoT gating. The key limitation we identified is the 鈥檆onfidently wrong鈥?scenario, where the model focuses on an incorrect region with high confidence (i.e., low entropy). As shown in Figure聽 11 , the model鈥檚 attention map is sharply localized on a specific region, yielding a low entropy value. Based on this signal, DA-CoT judges the model to be highly confident and, as designed, does not activate Chain-of-Thought. Consequently, the model performs a direct prediction based on its flawed initial grounding, leading to an incorrect answer. On the surface, this might appear to be an issue of incorrect reasoning path selection. However, our experiments confirmed that even when CoT was forcibly applied in such cases, the MLLM still failed to arrive at the correct answer. This suggests that the issue is less about 鈥榬easoning path selection鈥?and more related to a fundamental limitation in the MLLM鈥檚 own perceptual capabilities. This includes cases where the model initially misinterprets the query or image, or lacks the prerequisite knowledge about the scene. This analysis clarifies both the limitations and the scope of DA-CoT鈥檚 contribution. As a training-free framework, DA-CoT cannot expand a model鈥檚 fundamental perceptual abilities or directly correct its recognition errors. Instead, its core contribution lies in maximizing a model鈥檚 potential performance by preventing unnecessary reasoning and selectively activating CoT only when needed.
