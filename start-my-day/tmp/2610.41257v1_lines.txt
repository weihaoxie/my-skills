Abstract
Reasoning video object segmentation (ReVOS) requires segmenting the target specified by an implicit query throughout a video.
Existing MLLM-based methods typically require costly cross-model alignment, tying MLLM training to a downstream segmentation model.
We present ReVEAL , a decoupled thinking-with-video framework for ReVOS that elicits role-aware latent visual evidence from MLLM reasoning to directly guide segmentation.
ReVEAL introduces Visual Evidence Tokens (VETs) into video reasoning to associate necessary visual regions or entities with target, background, or distractor roles, while a Dynamic Attention Layer Router adaptively aggregates VET-to-video attention across layers into role-specific grounding evidence.
We train the MLLM to perform VET-augmented reasoning on our constructed ReVEAL-47K dataset, with a Contrastive Attention Grounding Loss as auxiliary supervision to apply role-specific constraints to VET attention and optimize layer routing for reliable latent grounding.
At inference, ReVEAL identifies the target through this thinking-with-video process and fuses the resulting role-specific evidence to guide a frozen SAM2 in predicting target masks.
Experiments on five benchmarks show that ReVEAL outperforms alignment-free baselines and remains competitive with alignment-based methods.

1 Introduction
Reasoning Video Object Segmentation (ReVOS) aims to segment target objects throughout a video according to free-form and often implicit language expressions. Unlike conventional referring video object segmentation (RVOS), the target may not be identifiable from a single frame or an explicit category name. Instead, it relies on the model鈥檚 ability to perceive and understand the video content in relation to the query, such as identifying 鈥渢he object used to keep the drink cold鈥?/em>. This makes Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)聽 ( Liu et al., 2023 ; Bai et al., 2023 ) a natural backbone for ReVOS. However, the challenge is not only to understand the expression, but also to connect such understanding to the visual evidence that supports a consistent object mask across time.
Since MLLMs do not directly produce pixel masks, existing MLLM-based ReVOS methods typically rely on cross-modal alignment with an external segmenter, making MLLM training dependent on the downstream segmentation model. Existing approaches often introduce a dedicated segmentation token, such as <SEG> , and align its hidden representation with the prompt space of an external segmenter聽 ( Kirillov et al., 2023 ; Ravi et al., 2025 ) .
Although straightforward for mask generation, such design incurs additional training costs and ties the framework to a specific MLLM鈥搒egmenter pair, complicating adaptation to alternative backbones and downstream settings.
Recent reasoning MLLMs adopt a thinking-with-images paradigm that incorporates visual primitives into intermediate chain-of-thoughts (CoT) steps, using boxes and points聽 ( OpenAI, 2025 ; Liu et al., 2025b ; Wang et al., 2025b ) or special tokens聽 ( Qin et al., 2025 ; Su et al., 2025 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) . This paradigm extends to thinking-with-video, where spatio-temporal cues serve as intermediate references for reasoning across frames聽 ( Zhang et al., 2025 ; Meng et al., 2025 ) . Interactive video segmentation models such as SAM2聽 ( Ravi et al., 2025 ) likewise accept spatial primitives as prompts for object identification and mask propagation, providing a natural interface through which visual evidence produced during MLLM reasoning can directly guide segmentation聽 ( Kao et al., 2025 ) .
Yet these primitives are typically limited to selected keyframes聽 ( Gong et al., 2025a ; Zhong et al., 2026 ) , providing only sparse grounding evidence across the video. Causal self-attention, on the other hand, offers a denser alternative聽 ( Vaswani et al., 2017 ) : text tokens attend to visual tokens across preceding frames, yielding video-wide attention maps with spatial grounding cues聽 ( Li et al., 2025 ) , although these cues may not reliably localize the corresponding regions聽 ( Jang et al., 2026 ) .
DecAF聽 ( Han et al., 2025 ) relies on a tailored multi-rollout procedure to obtain object and background attention maps for segmentation, incurring substantial inference cost without leveraging video reasoning itself.
This motivates us to explore whether an MLLM can produce the grounding evidence needed for segmentation as part of its own video reasoning within a single response.
Figure 1: Overview of ReVEAL. Previous methods mainly involve the segmenter during training or rely on multiple MLLM queries at inference to obtain grounding signals. In contrast, ReVEAL adopts a decoupled ReVOS framework that elicits internal visual evidence from the MLLM鈥檚 own video reasoning, enabling alignment-free integration with an external segmenter.
To this end, we present ReVEAL , a thinking-with-video framework for ReVOS that elicits role-aware latent visual evidence from MLLM reasoning to guide segmentation.
ReVEAL introduces Visual Evidence Tokens (VETs) to mark necessary visual regions or entities as target ( <OBJ> ), background ( <BG> ), or distractors ( <NEG> ) during reasoning.
A Dynamic Attention Layer Router (DALR) learns to adaptively aggregate their attention over preceding visual tokens across Transformer layers into role-specific evidence maps.
To enable the MLLM to elicit faithful role-aware latent visual evidence through video reasoning, we perform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for VET-augmented reasoning on our constructed ReVEAL-47K dataset, using a Contrastive Attention Grounding Loss (CAGL) as auxiliary supervision to optimize layer routing and apply role-specific constraints to VET attention over video tokens for reliable latent grounding.
At inference, ReVEAL identifies the target through VET-augmented video reasoning. DALR then aggregates VET-to-video attention from the CoT across Transformer layers into role-specific evidence.
These attention maps are then contrastively fused, and point prompts are sampled from high-confidence regions to guide a frozen external SAM2 in predicting the target masks, forming an alignment-free interface between MLLM reasoning and video object segmentation.
In a nutshell, our contributions are summarized as follows:
鈥?/span>
We present ReVEAL , a decoupled thinking-with-video framework that elicits role-aware latent visual evidence during MLLM reasoning to guide segmentation without cross-model alignment.
鈥?/span>
We equip the MLLM with this capability by combining reasoning supervision on ReVEAL-47K with CAGL for attention grounding over layers adaptively selected and weighted by DALR .
鈥?/span>
We demonstrate that internal signals from MLLM reasoning can provide effective grounding cues for video object segmentation through experiments on five RVOS and ReVOS benchmarks.
2 Related Work
2.1 Multimodal Large Language Models
Building on the success of LLMs in natural language processing, image MLLMs extend language models to visual inputs by bridging visual features into the language model through cross-attention resamplers聽 ( Alayrac et al., 2022 ) , querying transformers聽 ( Li et al., 2023 ; Dai et al., 2023 ) , or projection-based visual instruction tuning聽 ( Liu et al., 2023 ; Zhu et al., 2024 ) . Subsequent systems further scale visual backbones and training data to improve general vision鈥搇anguage capabilities聽 ( Chen et al., 2024b ; Bai et al., 2023 ; Li et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2024 ) . Video MLLMs extend this paradigm to video by incorporating sampled-frame representations and temporal modeling聽 ( Zhang et al., 2023 ; Maaz et al., 2024 ; Lin et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2026 ) . Beyond general video understanding, recent work increasingly adopts video MLLMs as general-purpose backbones for downstream tasks that require fine-grained perception and reasoning, including dense video captioning聽 ( Chen et al., 2024a ; Xu et al., 2024 ) , temporal grounding聽 ( Ren et al., 2024a ; Huang et al., 2024a ) , subject-specific video question answering聽 ( Yuan et al., 2025c ; Lian et al., 2025 ) , and embodied spatial reasoning聽 ( Yang et al., 2025 ) .
We extend this line of research to ReVOS by leveraging MLLMs鈥?spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities to identify target objects specified by complex queries.
2.2 Referring and Reasoning Video Object Segmentation
Referring Video Object Segmentation (RVOS) segments an object in a video based on a natural language expression. Early methods focus on explicit expressions through cross-modal fusion or query propagation聽 ( Seo et al., 2020 ; Botach et al., 2022 ; Wu et al., 2022 ) . Reasoning Video Object Segmentation (ReVOS) extends this task to implicit queries that identify targets through their functions, behaviors, or relations to other objects, requiring more complex reasoning聽 ( Yan et al., 2024 ) .
Most MLLM-based methods interpret implicit queries and learn cross-modal alignment by mapping the hidden state of a special <SEG> token to a downstream segmenter鈥檚 prompt space for mask prediction聽 ( Lai et al., 2024 ; Yan et al., 2024 ; Bai et al., 2024 ; Jin et al., 2026 ; Yuan et al., 2025a ; Lin et al., 2025 ; Gong et al., 2025b ) .
Recent work promotes this alignment indirectly through reinforcement learning, using SAM2-based segmentation rewards to optimize key-frame selection and MLLM-generated bounding boxes聽 ( Gong et al., 2025a ; Zhong et al., 2026 ) .
Both strategies tie MLLM training to a downstream segmenter, leaving the potential of native MLLM reasoning as an independent source of visual grounding evidence underexplored聽 ( Kao et al., 2025 ) .
In contrast, we train the MLLM under a thinking-with-video paradigm to elicit latent grounding evidence from video reasoning and use this evidence to guide an external segmenter at inference.
2.3 Visual Evidence in MLLM Reasoning
OpenAI-o3聽 ( OpenAI, 2025 ) introduces a thinking-with-images paradigm that incorporates references to visual regions into intermediate reasoning steps. Recent methods explicitly refer to these regions using boxes and points聽 ( Liu et al., 2025b ; Wang et al., 2025b ) or special tokens representing masks and image patches聽 ( Qin et al., 2025 ; Su et al., 2025 ; Yuan et al., 2025b ) .
This idea also extends to thinking-with-video, where spatio-temporal cues serve as references for video reasoning聽 ( Meng et al., 2025 ) .
However, generating such references throughout a video becomes cumbersome for dense video prediction. Instead, attention from output tokens to the preceding multimodal context provides a potential source of latent grounding evidence by highlighting visual regions associated with token semantics聽 ( Kang et al., 2025 ; Wu et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2025 ; Hu et al., 2026 ; Vaswani et al., 2017 ) .
Despite this potential, raw attention is often noisy and susceptible to attention sinks聽 ( Huang et al., 2024b ; Darcet et al., 2024 ) , limiting its reliability as visual evidence.
InterRVOS聽 ( Jin et al., 2026 ) constrains attention to relevant object regions to enrich semantic representations during cross-model alignment, whereas DecAF聽 ( Han et al., 2025 ) aggregates attention maps across multiple queries for visual grounding.
In contrast, ReVEAL derives role-aware latent visual evidence directly from video reasoning and fuses those cues for more accurate target localization.
3 Method
Reasoning video object segmentation (ReVOS) aims to segment the target specified by an implicit query throughout a video. We propose ReVEAL, a decoupled thinking-with-video framework that uses role-aware latent evidence from MLLM reasoning to guide segmentation.
During training, the MLLM learns to use VETs as role-specific semantic anchors for necessary visual regions or entities, with spatio-temporal grounding constraints applied directly to its own attention rather than through alignment with a downstream segmenter.
At inference, ReVEAL derives role-specific latent visual evidence through VET-augmented video reasoning and fuses this evidence as grounding signals to guide a frozen SAM2 in predicting target masks.

Training Data Construction.
To provide supervision for ReVEAL, we construct ReVEAL-47K , a training dataset comprising VET-augmented CoT traces and region-level mask annotations, from multiple training sources聽 ( Lai et al., 2024 ; Khoreva et al., 2018 ; Seo et al., 2020 ; Ding et al., 2023 ; Deng et al., 2025 ; Yan et al., 2024 ) . For each sample, we use GAR聽 ( Wang et al., 2025c ) with the GT mask on a representative frame to obtain a target caption. Conditioned on this caption, the query, and the video frames, Gemini 2.5 Pro聽 ( Comanici et al., 2025 ) generates a CoT response and inserts VETs at the corresponding positions. Qwen3VL聽 ( Bai et al., 2025 ) is used to verify and filter out unreliable samples. The mask annotations include the retained GT mask and a background mask, obtained by taking the complement of the union of the foreground regions predicted by BiRefNet聽 ( Zheng et al., 2024a ) and the target region. VET Fusion at Inference.
Table聽 7 compares different VET attention combinations from the same CoT at inference. Adding <BG> improves attention-mask scores by 3.9% and 4.6% on Ref-DAVIS and ReasonVOS, while adding <NEG> instead yields better SAM2 segmentation despite lower attention-mask scores. This suggests that background suppression refines the overall target coverage, whereas distractor suppression makes attention peaks more discriminative.
Combining all three VET types yields the best overall performance, supporting the effectiveness of our role design.
MLLM Backbone Scalability.
As shown in Table聽 7 , ReVEAL consistently outperforms the baseline across diverse MLLM backbones, demonstrating its generalizability.
We further observe that the performance generally improves with backbone size. For example, scaling Qwen3VL from 4B to 8B improves 饾挜 & 鈩?/mi> \mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F} by 3.6% on Ref-DAVIS and ReVOS and 5.6% on ReasonVOS, suggesting that larger MLLMs provide more reliable visual evidence for downstream segmentation.
Segmentation Backbone Scalability.
ReVEAL uses latent visual evidence from MLLM reasoning to guide segmentation without requiring alignment to a specific segmenter. As shown in Table聽 7 , the latent grounding evidence generalizes across smaller SAM2 variants and SAM3聽 ( Carion et al., 2026 ) on all three benchmarks, with minor performance variations due to the segmenter鈥檚 own capability.

5 Conclusion
We presented ReVEAL, a decoupled thinking-with-video framework for alignment-free ReVOS. ReVEAL enables the MLLM to identify targets through video reasoning and derive grounding cues from role-aware VET attention, without requiring cross-model alignment with the segmenter during training. At inference, ReVEAL derives role-specific evidence from VET-augmented video reasoning to guide a frozen SAM2 for target mask prediction. Experiments across five RVOS and ReVOS benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of ReVEAL. Overall, our work highlights the potential of internal signals from MLLM reasoning to bridge high-level video understanding and dense visual prediction.

Appendix A ReVEAL-47K Training Dataset
A.1 Dataset Overview
Figure 6: ReVEAL-47K Composition.
To equip the model with dynamic role-aware VET reasoning and reliable latent visual grounding, we construct ReVEAL-47K training dataset, comprising 47K VET-augmented ReVOS CoT traces paired with target and background masks for attention grounding.
ReVEAL-47K is constructed from the training splits of five public RVOS and ReVOS datasets, including MeViS聽 ( Ding et al., 2023 ) , ReVOS聽 ( Yan et al., 2024 ) , GroundMoRe聽 ( Deng et al., 2025 ) , Ref-DAVIS聽 ( Khoreva et al., 2018 ) , and Ref-YouTube-VOS聽 ( Seo et al., 2020 ) , together with the image reasoning segmentation dataset ReasonSeg聽 ( Lai et al., 2024 ) , as shown in Fig.聽 6 . We use six frames as the model input for all samples. For ReasonSeg, each sample is included twice, with its single image repeated across six frames and treated as a static video.
The resulting dataset contains 47K VET-augmented CoT annotations. Each trace contains exactly one <OBJ> token for target evidence, even when the query refers to multiple target objects, as its attention map can cover multiple target regions, while suppressive VETs are introduced only when needed. Specifically, <BG> appears at most once to provide background evidence, whereas multiple <NEG> tokens can be used to distinguish different distractors. On average, each trace contains 1.00 <OBJ> , 0.42 <BG> , and 0.86 <NEG> tokens.

D.1 More Comparisons with Other Methods
Figure聽 9 compares a representative case from ReasonVOS between ReVEAL and the Without-CoT paradigm.
In this example, ReVEAL improves attention-mask 饾挜 & 鈩?/mi> \mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F} by only 1.7% over Without-CoT, yet its VET attention consistently favors the true target despite some hallucinated responses, whereas Without-CoT favors a visually similar distractor. Attention-mask evaluation reflects the spatiotemporal coverage of regions retained after thresholding, whereas SAM2 uses point prompts sampled from high-confidence attention locations. A higher attention-mask score does not necessarily indicate more confident attention peaks on the target. Stronger attention peaks on the true target may therefore yield more reliable point prompts, helping explain the larger gain in SAM2 segmentation despite the modest improvement in attention-mask quality.

D.3 Failure Case Analysis and Limitations
Failure cases.
Figure聽 14 , 15 , and聽 16 show representative failures caused by limited target visibility and instance-level ambiguity. When the target is fully occluded, has not yet appeared, or is visible only briefly, limited visual and temporal context may lead to spurious attention activations in irrelevant regions. When multiple visually similar objects are present, the MLLM may struggle to identify the referred target during CoT reasoning, causing VET attention to focus on incorrect instances.
Figure 14: Representative failure case on MeViS_u, where the fast-moving hand appears in too few of the six uniformly sampled input frames to provide sufficient temporal and visual context.
Figure 15: Representative failure case where the VET attention maps fail to distinguish the target among a group of similar tigers.
Figure 16: Representative failure case where ReVEAL fails to distinguish the target from textual reasoning when numerous visually similar distractors are present.
Limitations.
Despite its effectiveness, ReVEAL has several limitations.
First, the current <NEG> design may not adequately distinguish individual distractors when multiple visually similar objects appear in more complex scenes.
Second, VET grounding can become unreliable when MLLM attention is diffuse or focused on irrelevant regions, particularly under severe occlusion.
Third, the current uniform sampling strategy may miss frames containing key visual entities, leaving them absent from the model input. The resulting lack of visual context may limit the model鈥檚 ability to reason effectively and produce reliable latent visual evidence.
Future work could explore lightweight optimizations centered on the MLLM鈥檚 own video reasoning for ReVOS. For example, a frame selection module inspired by GLUS聽 ( Lin et al., 2025 ) and CoT-RVS聽 ( Kao et al., 2025 ) could help retain informative frames. More broadly, we will explore extending this efficient MLLM-centric design to omni scenarios.
